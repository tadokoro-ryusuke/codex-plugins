# Indie Product Marketing

Build an evidence-backed route from a customer problem to retained, profitable demand. Treat marketing as customer learning and distribution design across the whole product lifecycle, not as promotion added after development.

If the user has no concrete opportunity, wants a broad list of app ideas, or needs to discover problems worth solving, use `$indie-idea-discovery` if available to establish a customer-problem hypothesis or product concept. Carry that context into any validation or marketing work already requested.

## Choose the operating mode

Select the narrowest mode that matches the request:

1. **Market research** — map customers, pains, substitutes, competitors, demand signals, and reachable channels.
2. **Demand validation** — design the cheapest ethical test that can falsify the riskiest assumption.
3. **Launch planning** — prepare positioning, instrumentation, seed users, assets, channel sequencing, and follow-up.
4. **Growth diagnosis** — locate the first broken stage from reach through referral and prioritize repairs.
5. **Channel experiment** — test one audience-message-offer-channel combination with a budget and decision rule.
6. **Marketing audit** — verify claims, metrics, channel fit, policy compliance, and unit economics.

Infer the mode from available context. Ask for missing information only when it materially changes the next action; otherwise state assumptions and proceed.

## Start with an evidence ledger

Separate every important statement into one of these classes:

- **Observed fact** — supplied analytics, customer records, product behavior, or a directly verified public fact.
- **Reported fact** — a founder, video, post, or case study reports it, but independent verification is unavailable.
- **Inference** — a conclusion derived from facts with explicit assumptions.
- **Hypothesis** — a plausible claim that still needs a test.
- **Recommendation** — an action justified by the preceding evidence.

Rank sources in this order:

1. Official platform documentation, laws, first-party analytics, and the user's raw data.
2. Peer-reviewed research and reproducible datasets.
3. Detailed first-party case studies with dates, denominators, and economics.
4. Practitioner heuristics and benchmark reports with disclosed methodology.
5. Anecdotes, screenshots without context, and generic advice.

Read [references/evidence-base.md](references/evidence-base.md) when validating marketing claims, the two source videos, ASO, SEO, AI discoverability, referrals, or paid-social advice. Never generalize one founder's CPA, conversion rate, or launch result into a universal benchmark.

## Build the product-market thesis

Reuse an existing product-market thesis. Establish or clarify the parts that affect the requested decision before recommending tactics:

- **Customer** — one narrow segment and the situation that triggers action.
- **Job and pain** — what progress they seek, current friction, urgency, and frequency.
- **Substitute** — what they use or do today, including doing nothing.
- **Promise** — the specific outcome, time horizon, and reason to believe.
- **Offer** — product, price, commitment, risk reversal, and primary call to action.
- **Distribution premise** — where the customer already pays attention or expresses intent.
- **Retention premise** — the recurring value that should bring the customer back.
- **Economic premise** — revenue model, gross contribution, service cost, and affordable acquisition cost.
- **Riskiest assumption** — the single belief most likely to invalidate the business.

For broad strategy work, use the marketing brief in `assets/marketing-brief.md` to capture a reusable thesis. Keep a narrow channel or metric diagnosis focused on its relevant assumptions.

## Route by lifecycle

Apply the guidance for the product's current stage and the requested scope. Use available customer evidence; do not make an interview or a full launch program a prerequisite for a bounded desk-research or experiment-design request. Prepare the requested artifacts and continue authorized work through verification; planned campaigns, proposed instrumentation, and observed outcomes are different evidence.

### Before building

1. Interview customers about recent behavior, not hypothetical interest.
2. Inspect search queries, competitor reviews, support threads, communities, and paid alternatives.
3. Test the riskiest assumption with the strongest affordable signal.
4. Prefer the signal ladder: page view < click < email < qualified conversation < prototype use < deposit or preorder < repeated use < paid retention.
5. Treat a waitlist as permission to continue learning, not proof of product-market fit.
6. Define pass, revise, and stop thresholds before collecting results.

### Before launch

1. Define the activation event and first-value path.
2. Define measurement for the launch decision, covering the relevant source, funnel, cohort, retention, revenue, refund, and support signals. Install it when implementation is in scope.
3. Recruit a small, concentrated seed cohort and observe them using the product.
4. Fix severe onboarding, reliability, privacy, and value-delivery failures.
5. Choose a primary channel; add secondary or owned follow-up channels when audience access and operating capacity justify them.
6. Plan launch actions and follow-up experiments over a window that can reveal the product's activation and natural usage cycle.

### After launch

Diagnose the first weak link in this order:

`qualified reach -> product-page visit -> signup/install -> activation -> retention -> revenue -> referral`

Repair the earliest material leak before buying more traffic. Segment every downstream metric by acquisition source; low-cost installs that do not activate or retain are not efficient growth.

### Before scaling

Require evidence of:

- Stable activation and cohort retention for the product's natural usage interval.
- A monetization path with positive gross contribution or a bounded, funded path to it.
- A channel whose retained-customer acquisition cost fits the economic model.
- Operational capacity for support, moderation, reliability, and policy compliance.
- A repeatable experiment rather than a single viral spike.

Read [references/field-playbook.md](references/field-playbook.md) for the full lifecycle workflow and product-type branches.

## Select channels from behavior, not fashion

Classify distribution into five systems:

1. **Capture existing demand** — SEO, ASO, marketplaces, directories, comparison pages, and intent-driven search ads.
2. **Create demand** — founder-led content, short-form video, education, communities, events, and public demonstrations.
3. **Borrow trust and audience** — partnerships, affiliates, creators, newsletters, integrations, and earned media.
4. **Buy attention** — paid social, paid search, sponsorships, and retargeting.
5. **Let the product propagate** — invitations, collaboration, user artifacts, referrals, embeds, templates, and attribution badges.

Score candidate channels by audience concentration, intent, message-medium fit, speed, compounding potential, measurability, cash requirement, founder fit, and policy risk. Start with one channel where the customer is concentrated and the product can be demonstrated naturally. Add channels only after learning why the first one works or fails.

Read [references/channel-strategy.md](references/channel-strategy.md) before producing channel-specific tactics, an organic-content plan, a paid campaign, an ASO/SEO plan, or a cold-start strategy.

## Design bounded experiments

Write every experiment with:

- Customer segment and triggering situation.
- One hypothesis in `because / therefore / expected observation` form.
- Control or baseline when feasible.
- One primary metric and no more than two guardrails.
- Attribution method and cohort definition.
- Minimum duration or sample requirement chosen before launch.
- Maximum spend and maximum founder hours.
- Pass, revise, stop, and follow-up rules.
- Notes for confounders, novelty effects, seasonality, and platform learning.

When no baseline exists, do not invent one. Run an instrumentation or feasibility pilot, use an absolute behavioral threshold justified by the business decision, and label the result exploratory. Use the first credible cohort as a future baseline only after checking source, product version, and measurement quality.

Do not call a one-day campaign conclusive when conversion volume is low. Do not change audience, message, creative, landing page, and offer simultaneously and then claim to know what caused the result. Keep an experiment record and compare retained value, not only clicks or installs.

Use `assets/experiment-backlog.md` for an experiment portfolio. Read [references/metrics-and-experiments.md](references/metrics-and-experiments.md) for formulas, instrumentation, reporting, and statistical guardrails.

## Treat product growth as product design

Design sharing around user value:

- Create an artifact users are proud or motivated to share.
- Preserve context so recipients understand the benefit immediately.
- Make the sender's benefit, recipient's benefit, and product's benefit compatible.
- Prefer user-initiated, personalized sharing over unsolicited automation.
- Measure share rate, recipient conversion, recipient activation, and retained referrals.
- Protect private data and let users preview what will be shared.

For network products, identify the smallest dense network that is useful. Seed real content, welcome newcomers, respond to early contributions, and make the product useful to one user when possible. Paid acquisition can seed liquidity, but it cannot repair an empty or low-value network.

## Calculate economics before declaring success

Use consistent denominators:

- `CPL = acquisition spend / qualified leads`
- `CAC = attributable sales and marketing cost / new paying customers`
- `CPI = acquisition spend / incremental installs`
- `activation rate = activated new users / eligible new users`
- `retention = active users from a cohort at interval N / eligible cohort users`
- `gross contribution = revenue - platform fees - variable infrastructure - support and fulfillment costs`
- `payback period = CAC / monthly gross contribution per customer`

Estimate lifetime value only when churn and gross margin are stable enough to support it. Show sensitivity ranges instead of a precise early-stage LTV. Reject universal statements such as “a good CPA is ¥100–¥300”; an acceptable acquisition cost depends on activation, retention, monetization, margin, payback, and cash constraints.

## Apply compliance and trust gates

Before recommending or executing a campaign:

- Disclose paid or incentivized endorsements clearly.
- State the waitlist's purpose and the intended use of collected contact data.
- Never fabricate users, rankings, reviews, testimonials, urgency, or product capabilities.
- Follow platform rules for automation, bulk replies, likes, follows, and messaging.
- Obtain required tracking consent and respect platform privacy requirements.
- Keep fake-door tests transparent at the point of commitment and provide a clear next step or refund.
- Review UGC moderation, copyright, trademark, age, safety, and reporting obligations for community products.

Read [references/ethics-and-compliance.md](references/ethics-and-compliance.md) for Japan-focused and platform-specific guardrails. Flag legal uncertainty and recommend qualified advice when material.

## Produce decision-ready outputs

Deliver the minimum useful set for the selected mode:

1. **Executive diagnosis** — the current lifecycle stage and first constraint.
2. **Evidence ledger** — facts, reported facts, inferences, hypotheses, and confidence.
3. **Customer and positioning thesis** — one focused audience-message-offer combination.
4. **Prioritized actions** — now, next, and later with reasons.
5. **Experiment cards** — metric, guardrail, budget, duration, and decision rule.
6. **Measurement plan** — event names, source fields, cohorts, and reporting cadence.
7. **Risks and compliance checks** — trust, policy, privacy, and operational constraints.
8. **Learning agenda** — what remains unknown and the cheapest way to learn it.

Use `assets/launch-plan.md` for launch work and `assets/growth-scorecard.md` for post-launch diagnosis. Read [references/case-studies.md](references/case-studies.md) only when analogies or examples materially improve the recommendation; copy mechanisms, not surface tactics or vanity metrics.

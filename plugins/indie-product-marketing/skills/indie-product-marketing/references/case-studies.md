## How to use cases

Use a case to identify a mechanism worth testing, not to import a result. For every analogy, record:

- Product type, market, geography, date, price, and founder advantages.
- Customer segment and triggering situation.
- Distribution surface and why the audience was concentrated there.
- Starting baseline, numerator, denominator, time window, and attribution quality.
- Activation, retention, revenue, contribution, and founder labor when disclosed.
- What was directly observed, what was founder-reported, and what is inferred.
- The smallest local experiment that could validate the mechanism.

Avoid survivorship bias. Visible launch stories rarely disclose every failed product, creative, channel, or cohort.

## SubscBox

Source: [creator video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hT1Vg1NK6Rk) and [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%82%B5%E3%83%96%E3%82%B9%E3%82%AFbox/id6763571602).

### Reported sequence

1. Test demand with a landing page and small paid campaigns.
2. Release an app with a clear consumer utility.
3. Use X advertising for an initial stream of visitors or installs.
4. Benefit from an organic X breakout.
5. Improve store presentation, sharing, social participation, and content discovery.

### Transferable mechanism

- Use a small, instrumented campaign to recruit learning participants and test message-audience fit.
- Make the utility legible in a visual or short demonstration.
- Treat store-page presentation as part of acquisition, not packaging added at the end.
- Make a useful result shareable when doing so protects privacy and benefits the recipient.

### Do not copy blindly

- Do not use the reported download, revenue, or CPA figures as benchmarks; public sources do not verify them.
- Reconcile the reported acquisition arithmetic: ¥4,200 divided by 60 is approximately ¥70, not ¥30.
- Do not assume a viral event is repeatable or attributable to one campaign.
- Do not mass-produce low-value AI pages or use aggressive social engagement.
- Do not optimize installs without source-level activation, retention, proceeds, and contribution.

## Nui Biyori

Source: [creator video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cLQWBGCOvvI) and [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/%E3%81%AC%E3%81%84%E6%97%A5%E5%92%8C/id6774039809).

### Reported sequence

1. Identify a narrow community and an unmet social need.
2. Test the proposition with small X and Instagram campaigns.
3. Recruit 10–20 waitlist users into a hands-on beta.
4. Seed the early network and personally care for participants.
5. Measure retention, payments, organic sharing, and feedback.
6. Continue product and marketing iteration after launch.

### Transferable mechanism

- Start a network product in the smallest dense community where participants can create value for each other.
- Convert weak intent into product use before declaring validation.
- Welcome, respond to, and manually connect early participants.
- Measure meaningful interactions and retained local density rather than total registrations alone.
- Treat paid traffic as timed seeding, not a substitute for network value.

### Do not copy blindly

- The reported 40,000 users are not publicly defined as installs, registrations, active users, or retained users.
- Revenue was reported, but approximately 300 supporters at ¥300 per month produced less than the reported ¥150,000–¥180,000 infrastructure cost. This is growth with negative contribution, not a proven profitable business.
- Feedback volume can include duplicate requests, defects, and support demand; it is not PMF by itself.
- A target of 100,000 users or ¥10 million monthly revenue is an aspiration until assumptions and cohorts support it.

## Buffer

Source: [Idea to Paying Customers in 7 Weeks](https://buffer.com/resources/idea-to-paying-customers-in-7-weeks-how-we-did-it/).

### Transferable mechanism

- Use a minimal landing experience to test whether the promise earns action.
- Add pricing before building the full product to distinguish curiosity from willingness to pay.
- Speak directly with early users and build the narrow path they are trying to complete.
- Treat actual payment as stronger evidence than clicks or emails.

### Boundary

The case is a founder-authored retrospective from a different platform era. A pricing-page click can still be curiosity; the stronger evidence was a real paying customer and continued use.

## Dropbox

Source: [founder talk transcript](https://ecorner.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/05/2988.pdf).

### Transferable mechanism

- Demonstrate a technically difficult product before the full experience is broadly available.
- Place community-specific cues in the demonstration so a concentrated expert audience recognizes that the product understands them.
- Distribute where the exact early adopter already gathers.

### Boundary

The reported waitlist jump from 5,000 to 75,000 validates audience-message-demonstration fit, not retention or business economics. Copy the specificity of the demonstration, not the idea that every product needs a launch video.

## Tally

Source: [bootstrapped journey to $100K MRR](https://blog.tally.so/our-bootstrapped-journey-towards-100k-mrr/).

### Transferable mechanism

- Use a generous free product when marginal cost and upgrade logic support it.
- Turn responsive founder support into research, trust, and activation improvements.
- Ship repeatedly and compound product quality, templates, word of mouth, and launch exposure.
- Pair product-led distribution with a clear paid reason to upgrade.

### Boundary

The outcome accumulated across years and many releases. Do not compress it into an overnight-launch narrative or assume freemium works when free-user cost is high.

## Plausible

Source: [Plausible's path to $1M ARR](https://plausible.io/blog/open-source-saas).

### Transferable mechanism

- Take a differentiated position around a real customer concern.
- Make the product and business model credible through transparent technical work.
- Publish content that explains the category problem and helps the target customer decide.
- Let open source, community trust, and customer recommendations reinforce one another.

### Boundary

Open source is not a universal acquisition channel. It helps when technical users value inspectability and the commercial product has a clear reason to exist.

## Cross-case patterns

The cases converge on these mechanisms:

1. **Concentration before scale:** find a narrow audience or community where the problem is visible.
2. **Demonstration before abstraction:** show the product or outcome in the medium the audience understands.
3. **Stronger signals over large numbers:** progress from attention to use, payment, retention, and referral.
4. **Founder contact as research:** personally support early users and turn their friction into product changes.
5. **Product and distribution co-design:** store pages, documentation, templates, sharing, and onboarding all affect acquisition.
6. **Compounding after the first spike:** lasting businesses improve through many product, message, and channel iterations.
7. **Economics as a separate gate:** users and revenue do not prove positive contribution or scalable acquisition.

## Analogy checklist

Before recommending a case-derived tactic, answer:

- Is the target customer's motivation similar?
- Is the channel used for the same behavior and level of intent?
- Can this product be demonstrated or shared in the same way?
- Are price, margin, conversion delay, and repeat-use interval comparable?
- Does the founder have similar credibility, skills, access, or community permission?
- Are platform rules and algorithms still current?
- What hidden work, cost, or failure could explain the result?
- What observation would disconfirm the analogy quickly?

If three or more answers are unknown, present the tactic as an exploratory test rather than a recommendation.

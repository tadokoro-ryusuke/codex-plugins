## How to use this reference

Use this file to verify a claim before placing it in a strategy. Preserve the distinction between:

- The creator's observed or self-reported result.
- Independent evidence that the product, policy, or mechanism exists.
- Evidence that the mechanism generalizes to another product.
- A recommendation that still requires a local test.

Treat timestamps as navigation aids, not verbatim quotations. The YouTube captions are automatically generated and can mistranscribe numbers or product names. Check the video or creator's raw analytics before relying on a material number.

## Evidence grades

| Grade | Meaning | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| A | Official rules, law, first-party raw analytics, or a randomized/strong causal study | Establish a rule or support a mechanism |
| B | Peer-reviewed observational research, transparent datasets, or detailed first-party business records | Support a likely pattern with limitations |
| C | Founder case study with useful denominators but limited independent verification | Generate hypotheses and analogies |
| D | Anecdote, heuristic, unexplained benchmark, or unsupported assertion | Test only; do not present as fact |

### Mechanism claims

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence and correction |
|---|---|---|
| Use paid acquisition for fast initial demand or network seeding | Conditionally supported | Paid acquisition can buy a controlled stream of visitors and seed an empty product, but clicks or installs alone do not establish demand. Measure qualified conversion, activation, and retention by source. |
| Marketing is repeated testing, and the best channel varies by product | Supported | Platform experiment tools and experimentation research support hypothesis-driven tests. A one-day or low-conversion test is usually directional, not conclusive. |
| Match the medium to the product, such as motion creative for games | Plausible | Creative-product fit is a sound hypothesis, but static versus video performance must be tested within the actual audience and offer. |
| Grow a new social account through daily likes, comments, and follows | High policy risk if aggressive | Genuine, relevant conversation can work. X prohibits aggressive, bulk, indiscriminate, or automated engagement intended to drive attention. Manual execution does not make spam compliant. |
| Avoid fully automated AI replies and relationship content | Supported by trust and policy concerns | X requires prior written approval for AI reply bots and forbids automated likes and aggressive automated following. Human review and relevance remain necessary. |
| Build share features and shareable product moments | Supported | Randomized research shows that viral product features can cause peer adoption. Measure recipient value and retained referral conversion rather than share count alone. |
| Mass-produce dozens of AI-written SEO articles per day | Contradicted when pages add little value | Google's generative-AI guidance says scaled pages without user value may violate its scaled-content-abuse policy. Use AI for research and structure, then add original evidence, tools, examples, and editorial review. |
| SEO normally needs months | Directionally reasonable, not a rule | Indexing and ranking depend on crawlability, competition, authority, content usefulness, and demand. Do not promise a fixed two-to-three-month outcome. |
| Optimize for LLM recommendations mainly through metadata | Incomplete | ChatGPT Search requires crawl access for inclusion, while research on generative-engine visibility also studies citations, statistics, source quality, and content structure. No metadata-only recipe guarantees recommendation. |
| Improve ASO through screenshots, keywords, category, ratings, and reviews | Supported | Apple names metadata relevance and user behavior as discovery factors and provides product-page experimentation. |
| Frequent app updates improve search rank | Unverified | Apple does not name update frequency as a primary search-ranking factor on its discovery page. Updates may improve quality, reviews, or conversion indirectly. Treat the direct ranking claim as a hypothesis. |

### Transferable mechanism

The most defensible sequence is:

1. Find a narrow community with a specific social or emotional constraint.
2. Turn the problem into a focused proposition and quick representation.
3. Buy a small amount of targeted traffic to test message and recruit interviews.
4. Convert a subset into hands-on testers before public launch.
5. Seed the network and personally welcome or respond to early contributions.
6. Instrument activation, retention, payment, and organic sharing.
7. Improve product and distribution in parallel until pull is repeatable.
8. Fix unit economics before broad platform or geography expansion.

Do not copy the surface tactic of “run X ads.” Copy the staged reduction of uncertainty and the intensive care of a concentrated early network.

## Authoritative platform evidence

### Paid and social platforms

- [X Ads eligibility](https://business.x.com/en/help/ads-policies/campaign-considerations/about-eligibility-for-x-ads): advertising accounts must meet policy, status, profile-quality, country, and verification requirements. The video's verification warning is current, but price and product details can change.
- [X automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation): automated likes are prohibited; aggressive or indiscriminate automated following is prohibited; AI reply bots require prior written approval.
- [X authenticity policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/authenticity): bulk unsolicited replies, engagement spam, follow churn, and aggressive attention-seeking engagement are prohibited, including some manual behavior.
- [Meta Advantage+ audience](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/meta-advantage-plus/audience): automated expansion can seek lower-cost outcomes, but strict location, language, age, and exclusions remain available.
- [Meta ad budgets and schedules](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/pricing): Meta advises enough budget over at least seven days so delivery can learn; this contradicts treating every one-day result as stable.
- [Google Ads experiment guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7281575): define a business-linked hypothesis, keep experiment records, and avoid changing the base campaign during the test.
- [Google Ads experiment FAQ](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13826584): multiple concurrent experiments can interfere; high-volume campaigns and longer windows improve experiment power.

### Search and app-store discovery

- [Google guidance on generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content): use AI for research and structure, but avoid scaled pages without added value.
- [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies): scaled content abuse is large amounts of unoriginal or low-value content created primarily to manipulate ranking, regardless of how it is produced.
- [Apple App Store search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/): title, subtitle, keywords, category, downloads, ratings, reviews, product-page clarity, and localization contribute to discovery and conversion.
- [Apple product-page optimization](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/create-product-page-optimization-tests/overview-of-product-page-optimization/): test up to three icon, screenshot, or preview variants with randomized exposure and conversion analysis.
- [Apple acquisition analytics](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/acquisition/): compare conversion, downloads, usage, sales, and subscriptions by search, browse, app referrer, web referrer, and campaign.
- [Apple ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/): request reviews at appropriate satisfaction moments and use the platform prompt rather than coercion.

### AI discovery

- [OpenAI ChatGPT Search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search): allow OAI-SearchBot and its published IPs for potential inclusion; inclusion does not guarantee ranking or recommendation.
- [OpenAI publishers and developers FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856): allowing OAI-SearchBot enables possible summaries/snippets and measurable referral traffic.
- [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735): introduces experimental visibility measures and tests content changes such as citations, quotations, and statistics. Results are engine-, query-, and domain-dependent.

### Privacy and disclosure in Japan

- [Consumer Affairs Agency stealth-marketing FAQ](https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/representation/fair_labeling/faq/stealth_marketing/): advertiser-controlled endorsements must be recognizable as advertising; product provision can require clear disclosure.
- [Personal Information Protection Commission guidelines](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/legal/guidelines_tsusoku/): when collecting email addresses through a prepared form, specify the purpose of use as required.
- [Apple App Tracking Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/apptrackingtransparency): request authorization when sharing user or device data for cross-app or cross-site tracking.

## Research evidence

- [Aral and Walker, Creating Social Contagion Through Viral Product Design](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1421): a randomized Facebook field experiment involving 9,687 experimental users and about 1.4 million friends found causal differences between active personalized messages and passive broadcast features. Personalized messages were more effective per message; passive features generated more total adoption because they were used more often.
- [Berger and Milkman, What Makes Online Content Viral?](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353): observational analysis found that high-arousal emotion and positive content were associated with greater sharing. Do not turn this into manipulative emotional copy; use it to explain why useful, surprising, identity-relevant artifacts may spread.
- [Joyce and Kraut, Predicting Continued Participation in Newsgroups](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/3/723/4617705): newcomers who received a reply were more likely to post again, supporting active welcome and response in early communities.
- [Gimmon and Levie, Early Indicators of Very Long-Term Venture Performance](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amd.2019.0056): in 142 high-technology ventures, early sales traction predicted long-term survival and survival at scale. Early users without sales are a weaker business signal.
- [Giardino et al., Why Early-Stage Software Startups Fail](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04749): multiple-case research describes failure when teams espouse problem/solution learning but execution prioritizes product development and neglects learning.
- [Microsoft, A Dirty Dozen](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/a-dirty-dozen-twelve-common-metric-interpretation-pitfalls-in-online-controlled-experiments/): documents recurring experiment-interpretation failures observed across thousands of software experiments.
- [Referral Programs for Platform Growth](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/112008): randomized platform evidence supports testing referral incentives, not assuming they always produce healthy growth.

## Practitioner cases

- [Buffer: Idea to Paying Customers in 7 Weeks](https://buffer.com/resources/idea-to-paying-customers-in-7-weeks-how-we-did-it/): a pricing page and conversations preceded a rough product; the stronger evidence was a paying customer within days, not page visits alone.
- [Dropbox founder talk transcript](https://ecorner.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2012/05/2988.pdf): a demonstration tailored to an existing community reportedly grew the beta list from 5,000 to 75,000 overnight. The mechanism was concentrated audience-message fit and a demonstrable product, not “make any video.”
- [Tally's path to $100K MRR](https://blog.tally.so/our-bootstrapped-journey-towards-100k-mrr/): a generous free product, personal support, launch visibility, and product-led distribution compounded over more than 150 updates. It was not an overnight launch.
- [Plausible's path to $1M ARR](https://plausible.io/blog/open-source-saas): differentiated positioning, transparent open source, content, community trust, and word of mouth produced organic growth without paid advertising. This depended on a strong category narrative and technical credibility.
- [First Round on measuring PMF](https://review.firstround.com/how-to-measure-product-market-fit/): the Sean Ellis “very disappointed” survey is a leading indicator and segmentation tool, not a substitute for retention, revenue, or market size.

## What remains uncertain

Recheck these items when they materially affect a plan:

- Current platform eligibility, ad objectives, minimum budgets, and automation policies.
- The two videos' raw campaign exports, install attribution, cohort retention, revenue, refunds, and platform fees.
- Whether the 40,000 Nui Biyori users are registered users, installs, monthly active users, or another definition.
- The direct causal source of the organic breakout after initial paid seeding.
- Current SEO and generative-search behavior for the user's language, market, and query set.
- Whether observed ratings were reset between app versions or reflect the full lifetime rating count.
- Product-specific legal obligations, especially UGC moderation, children's access, promotions, health/finance claims, and international tracking.

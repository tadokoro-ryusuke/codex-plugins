## Channel selection score

Score each candidate from 1–5 and state the evidence behind the score:

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Audience concentration | Does a large share of the exact segment gather here? |
| Intent | Are users already seeking a solution or only consuming entertainment? |
| Message-medium fit | Can the value be demonstrated naturally in this format? |
| Speed | How quickly can a credible signal arrive? |
| Compounding | Does today's work produce durable discovery, trust, or referrals? |
| Measurability | Can source, activation, retention, and revenue be tied back? |
| Cash requirement | Can the founder afford enough volume to learn? |
| Founder fit | Can the founder sustain the required sales, writing, video, or community work? |
| Control | Can the founder retain an owned audience or direct relationship? |
| Policy risk | Could tactics violate platform, privacy, advertising, or community rules? |

Do not simply select the highest sum. Eliminate channels that fail a hard constraint, then choose one fast-learning channel and one compounding or owned channel.

## Distribution systems

### Capture existing demand

Use when the customer already names or searches for the problem.

- Search ads.
- SEO pages answering a real task or comparison.
- App Store / Play Store search.
- Marketplaces, package registries, directories, and integration stores.
- Competitor-alternative and switching pages.

Primary risk: demand may be small, expensive, or controlled by incumbents.

### Create demand

Use when customers feel the pain but do not search for the category.

- Founder-led education.
- Demonstrations and before/after workflows.
- Short-form video and visual stories.
- Build-in-public content tied to customer outcomes.
- Events, webinars, workshops, and community participation.

Primary risk: attention without intent. Always provide a next meaningful action and measure qualified progression.

### Borrow trust and audience

Use when another product, expert, or community already has the target relationship.

- Integrations and co-marketing.
- Affiliates and referral partners.
- Creator demonstrations.
- Newsletter sponsorships or guest content.
- Earned media and curated launches.

Primary risk: poor audience alignment or dependence on one gatekeeper. Disclose incentives.

### Buy attention

Use for message testing, predictable traffic, retargeting, or accelerating a proven funnel.

- Paid social.
- Paid search.
- Sponsorships.
- Display or app-install campaigns.

Primary risk: optimizing cheap proxy events while downstream value remains weak.

### Let the product propagate

Use when normal product use creates an artifact, collaborator, audience, or recipient.

- Invitations and collaboration.
- Shareable results, reports, pages, or media.
- Templates and public galleries.
- Embeds and “made with” attribution.
- Referral rewards aligned with retained value.

Primary risk: spam, privacy leakage, low-recipient value, or incentives that attract fraud.

## Paid acquisition

### Choose the campaign objective from the learning goal

| Learning goal | Better objective | Weak proxy to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Test message/audience resonance | Qualified landing-page action | Impressions alone |
| Recruit interviews | Completed screener and booked interview | Email without qualification |
| Acquire app users | Install plus downstream activation where supported | Web click counted as an install |
| Acquire paying users | Purchase or qualified trial with cohort tracking | Cheapest signup |
| Seed a network | Activated contributor or successful interaction | Broad install count |

### Run a small campaign correctly

1. Define one segment, one promise, one offer, and one creative concept.
2. Set source parameters and verify event firing before spend.
3. Predeclare the budget cap and directional versus decision-grade target.
4. Allow platform learning and natural conversion delay.
5. Check lead quality and downstream behavior manually.
6. Calculate cost per qualified, activated, retained, and paying user.
7. Record creative, audience, placements, dates, exclusions, and confounders.

Small budgets can falsify a clearly bad message or recruit initial interviews. They often cannot distinguish modest variants with statistical confidence. Label results accordingly.

### Scale with marginal economics

Increase budget in steps. Monitor whether the next cohort has worse CPM, CTR, conversion, activation, retention, refund, or payback. Average historic CAC can conceal deteriorating marginal CAC.

## Organic social and communities

### Use a value-first participation loop

1. Identify recurring questions and work artifacts in the target community.
2. Help with the problem without forcing a link.
3. Disclose affiliation when discussing the product.
4. Publish demonstrations, original data, useful templates, or lessons.
5. Invite relevant users into a direct conversation or owned follow-up.
6. Record which topic, format, and community drives activated users.

Avoid generic daily posting calendars detached from customer behavior. A smaller number of high-specificity demonstrations can outperform high volume.

### Separate relationship work from content assistance

Use AI for research, outlines, repurposing, transcription, and draft variants. Require human review for facts, tone, disclosure, and direct replies. Do not automate likes, follows, unsolicited replies, or bulk DMs where platform rules prohibit them.

### Use build in public carefully

Share customer learning, constraints, tradeoffs, useful technical lessons, or verified outcomes. Do not make the founder journey the only reason a target customer should care. Separate peer-founder engagement from buyer demand in analytics.

## SEO and content

### Build a demand map before pages

Classify queries by:

- Problem awareness.
- Solution/category awareness.
- Comparison and alternative intent.
- Implementation task.
- Switching or migration intent.
- Brand/navigation intent.

Prioritize queries where the product can provide the best next step and where original evidence or functionality exists.

### Create a useful content unit

Prefer one or more of:

- Original data with methodology.
- A working calculator, template, or checker.
- A tested workflow with screenshots and limitations.
- A current comparison with clear selection criteria.
- A case study with baseline, intervention, result, and caveats.
- Documentation that gets a user to a successful result.

Use generative AI to accelerate research and structure, not to publish dozens of undifferentiated pages. Review for accuracy, originality, freshness, product fit, and a helpful next action.

### Measure the full content cohort

Track indexed pages, qualified impressions, visits, CTA progression, activation, revenue, assisted conversion, update cost, and content decay. Rankings and sessions alone are insufficient.

## ASO and app stores

### Separate discoverability from conversion

**Discoverability inputs**

- Accurate name, subtitle, keywords, and category.
- Localization.
- Downloads, ratings, reviews, and user behavior.
- In-app events and eligible store features.

**Conversion inputs**

- Recognizable icon.
- First screenshots or preview communicating outcome and proof.
- Accurate subtitle and description.
- Trust, reviews, compatibility, privacy, and pricing clarity.

### Use store-native experiments

On Apple platforms, use Product Page Optimization to randomize icons, screenshots, and previews. Choose one hypothesis per treatment, run until the platform provides meaningful confidence, and compare downstream quality by source where possible.

Do not claim an update-frequency ranking effect without evidence. Update to improve value, quality, ratings, localization, and conversion, then measure the actual impact.

## Partnerships and borrowed audiences

### Select by shared customer workflow

Strong partners have:

- The same target user but a complementary product.
- A natural handoff or combined outcome.
- Trust with the user.
- Comparable quality and disclosure standards.
- A measurable exchange of value.

Start with a small joint artifact, integration, webinar, migration guide, or customer workflow. Track partner-sourced activation and retained revenue, not only referred traffic.

### Structure incentives

Tie affiliate or referral reward to a quality event such as paid conversion after a refund window, not raw signups. Disclose the relationship and protect against self-referral, fraud, and low-fit volume.

## Product-led and referral loops

### Define the loop explicitly

`core use -> valuable artifact or collaboration -> recipient exposure -> recipient value -> recipient activation -> new core use`

For each arrow, define a metric and likely friction.

### Design shareable moments

Make the artifact:

- Useful or expressive without requiring product knowledge.
- Understandable in a feed or message preview.
- Safe to share, with a preview and privacy controls.
- Branded lightly enough to preserve user value.
- Deep-linked to the relevant recipient experience.

Do not add a generic share button and assume a loop exists. Compare share initiation, completion, recipient click, recipient activation, and retained referral.

### Choose active or passive propagation

- **Active personalized:** the user selects recipients or writes context. Usually higher intent and adoption per message.
- **Passive broadcast:** normal use exposes attribution or activity. Usually higher volume but lower intent.

Test both only where they respect user expectations.

## Network-product cold start

### Find the atomic network

Define the smallest group, geography, niche, or workflow in which supply and demand create recurring value. Optimize local density before total signups.

Examples of density metrics:

- Meaningful posts per active viewer.
- Percentage of newcomers receiving a relevant reply.
- Successful matches per seeker.
- Time to first response.
- Percentage of listings receiving a qualified inquiry.
- Percentage of users with at least one relevant connection.

### Seed without deception

- Contribute founder-owned content under a real identity.
- Recruit power users from one existing community with permission.
- Import or curate licensed/public data where allowed.
- Manually match or welcome users.
- Make a single-player utility useful before the network matures.

Paid traffic can bring the first participants at a predictable time, but spend should target contributors or successful interactions, not empty registrations.

## AI discovery

### Establish technical eligibility

- Allow the relevant search crawler when inclusion is desired.
- Serve crawlable, canonical, fast, and accessible pages.
- Use accurate titles, headings, structured semantics, and stable URLs.
- Keep product, pricing, compatibility, and documentation current.

### Improve sourceworthiness

- Publish original data, clear methodology, definitions, and dated updates.
- Cite primary sources.
- State limitations and avoid unsupported superlatives.
- Earn legitimate mentions and reviews from relevant independent sources.
- Make important claims concise and unambiguous.

### Measure rather than promise

Track a fixed query set across engines and dates, citations or mentions, referral sessions, assisted conversions, and inaccuracies. Generative answers vary, and no schema or `llms.txt` file guarantees inclusion or recommendation.

## Channel portfolio rules

Use this sequence:

1. One fast-learning channel to test segment-message-offer fit.
2. One owned channel to preserve the relationship.
3. One compounding channel aligned with repeated customer questions or product propagation.
4. One scalable paid or partner channel only after activation, retention, and economics support it.

Retire channels that repeatedly fail their predeclared quality or economic threshold. Do not preserve a tactic because it once produced a viral spike.

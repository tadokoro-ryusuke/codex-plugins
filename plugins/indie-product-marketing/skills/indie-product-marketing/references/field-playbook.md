## Operating principles

1. Start with a behavior and a problem, not a feature list.
2. Let a strong signal buy the right to run the next, more expensive test.
3. Separate acquisition, activation, retention, monetization, and referral.
4. Compare cohorts by source; aggregate growth hides low-quality acquisition.
5. Keep the founder close to early customers until patterns stabilize.
6. Build one repeatable channel before constructing a broad channel portfolio.
7. Measure contribution and founder time, not only revenue or users.
8. Predeclare stop conditions so sunk cost and hope do not control decisions.

## Phase 0: frame the business

### Create the constraint sheet

Record:

- Product type and lifecycle stage.
- Geography, language, device, and platform.
- Available founder hours per week.
- Cash budget and maximum acceptable loss.
- Existing audience, distribution assets, relationships, and brand trust.
- Regulatory, privacy, moderation, safety, or platform constraints.
- Revenue model, price, gross margin, and major variable costs.
- Current data quality and analytics coverage.

### Define the target situation

Use this sentence:

> When **[specific person]** experiences **[triggering situation]**, they currently **[substitute behavior]**, which causes **[measurable or emotional cost]**. The product helps them achieve **[outcome]** through **[distinct mechanism]**.

Reject segments such as “everyone who wants productivity.” Narrow by trigger, context, current substitute, urgency, and reachability.

## Phase 1: discover the problem

### Build a research map

Collect evidence from:

- Recent customer interviews.
- Competitor reviews sorted by recent, critical, and most helpful.
- Search queries and adjacent questions.
- Community threads where the problem appears without prompting.
- Support requests and product analytics if a product exists.
- Alternatives people already pay for.
- Workarounds, spreadsheets, manual services, or repeated scripts.
- Market or platform changes that create urgency.

### Run behavior-based interviews

Ask about the most recent instance:

1. “Tell me about the last time this happened.”
2. “What triggered you to deal with it then?”
3. “What did you try first?”
4. “What did that cost in time, money, risk, or frustration?”
5. “Who else was involved in the decision?”
6. “What did you like and dislike about the current solution?”
7. “What happens if you do nothing?”
8. “Where did you look for a solution?”
9. “What made you trust or reject an option?”
10. “May I observe the workflow or see a non-sensitive artifact?”

Avoid asking “Would you use this?” or pitching too early. Intentions are cheap; recent behavior, paid alternatives, and access to artifacts are stronger.

### Synthesize without averaging away the niche

Tag notes by:

- Trigger.
- Frequency.
- Severity.
- Existing spend.
- Workaround sophistication.
- Decision authority.
- Channel or discovery path.
- Exact language used to describe the pain and desired outcome.

Look for a reachable cluster with repeated behavior. Do not combine incompatible segments into one persona.

## Phase 2: validate demand

### Test one assumption at a time

Common risky assumptions include:

- The problem occurs often enough.
- The customer recognizes and prioritizes it.
- The promise is understandable.
- The segment can be reached at an affordable cost.
- The product can deliver value quickly.
- Users return at the required frequency.
- A buyer will pay enough to cover delivery and acquisition.
- A network can reach useful local density.

### Climb the signal ladder

Use the cheapest level that can falsify the current assumption:

| Level | Test | What it can support | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Search, review, and community evidence | Problem language and existing behavior | Purchase intent |
| 2 | Customer interview or workflow observation | Severity, context, and substitutes | Scalable demand |
| 3 | Message/creative click test | Audience-message resonance | Product value or retention |
| 4 | Waitlist with qualifying fields | Willingness to identify and continue | Willingness to pay |
| 5 | Demo request or scheduled call | Higher intent and reachable buyer | Repeat usage |
| 6 | Concierge or Wizard-of-Oz delivery | Outcome value and workflow fit | Automated product economics |
| 7 | Deposit, preorder, or paid pilot | Willingness to commit money | Retention at scale |
| 8 | Repeated usage and renewal | Ongoing value | Scalable acquisition |

### Design an ethical landing-page test

Include:

- One customer and one outcome.
- The painful situation in customer language.
- A believable mechanism or demo.
- Scope and current availability status.
- Price or a meaningful commitment when price risk is material.
- One primary call to action.
- A privacy notice and clear use of contact data.
- A success page that states what happens next.

Track qualified sessions, CTA rate, completion rate, lead quality, booked conversation rate, and later activation. A cheap lead is not useful if the visitor misunderstood the offer.

### Make the decision

- **Pass:** the predicted segment takes the predicted meaningful action at a viable acquisition range.
- **Revise:** interest exists but the segment, problem, message, price, or delivery assumption is wrong.
- **Stop:** repeated tests with credible traffic and clear messaging fail the predeclared threshold, or economics cannot work.
- **Escalate evidence:** move from clicks to interviews, prototype use, payment, or retention before increasing build cost.

## Phase 3: prepare the product and launch

### Define the value path

Write:

- Entry promise.
- Required setup.
- Activation event.
- Time to first value.
- Habit or recurring trigger.
- Natural retention interval.
- Monetization moment.
- Share or collaboration moment.

Remove setup that does not help reach first value. Instrument every transition.

### Recruit a seed cohort

Select users who share a dense context, not a random crowd. For each seed user:

- Explain why they were invited.
- Observe first use.
- Personally resolve blockers.
- Ask what outcome they expected and whether it occurred.
- Record the next natural use occasion.
- Invite contribution or sharing only after value is delivered.

For community or marketplace products, prepopulate legitimate content, supply, or workflows. Do not fabricate users or activity.

### Prepare launch assets

Create:

- Positioning statement and short pitch.
- Product demo or proof of outcome.
- Landing/product-store page and source-specific variants.
- FAQ, privacy, terms, support, and known limitations.
- Founder story only where it helps the customer understand the product.
- Email sequence for testers, waitlist, launch, activation, and follow-up.
- Channel-native posts and partner briefing.
- UTM and campaign naming plan.
- Incident, support, and feedback routing.

### Set launch gates

Do not launch broadly until:

- The core path works reliably.
- Analytics identify acquisition source and activation.
- Privacy and tracking consent are correct.
- The team can respond to support and safety reports.
- Store/page copy matches the actual product.
- The seed cohort demonstrates at least the expected first value.

## Phase 4: launch and learn

### Use a launch sequence

**T-14 to T-7 days**

- Finalize seed-user findings and positioning.
- Prepare assets and partner/community permission.
- Verify analytics, store metadata, payments, support, and rollback.
- Capture a baseline for existing traffic and conversion.

**T-6 to T-1 days**

- Brief existing testers and waitlist members.
- Schedule only the assets that do not require live context.
- Rehearse the demo and support responses.
- Confirm platform rules and disclosure labels.

**Launch day**

- Notify the highest-intent owned audience first.
- Publish in the primary channel with a native demonstration.
- Respond to real questions and friction quickly.
- Record source, qualitative feedback, defects, and unusual behavior.
- Avoid declaring victory from impressions or ranking spikes.

**T+1 to T+7 days**

- Fix activation and reliability blockers.
- Segment activation and early retention by source.
- Interview converters and qualified non-converters.
- Repurpose the best validated message into the secondary channel.

**T+8 to T+30 days**

- Run bounded message, onboarding, pricing, product-page, and channel tests.
- Review cohort retention at the natural interval.
- Update the product roadmap from repeated outcomes, not request counts alone.
- Publish useful proof, learning, or customer outcomes without fabricated social proof.

## Phase 5: diagnose post-launch growth

### Find the first constraint

| Stage | Diagnostic question | Likely repair |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified reach | Are the right people seeing the offer? | Narrow audience, choose a denser channel, partner, or capture intent |
| Visit | Does the message earn attention from that audience? | Improve problem framing, proof, creative-medium fit, or promise |
| Signup/install | Is the commitment worth the expected value and trust? | Clarify scope, price, risk, trust, compatibility, and CTA |
| Activation | Do new users reach meaningful value quickly? | Remove setup, improve onboarding, templates, data import, or concierge help |
| Retention | Does value recur at the expected interval? | Fix use case, reminders, workflow integration, quality, or segment fit |
| Revenue | Does the buyer pay and remain profitable? | Adjust packaging, price, payer, costs, or monetization timing |
| Referral | Does sharing create value for sender and recipient? | Create a useful artifact, invitation, collaboration, or fair incentive |

### Use mixed evidence for PMF

Review together:

- Retention curve and cohort stability.
- Frequency and depth of the core behavior.
- Paid conversion, renewal, refunds, and expansion.
- Organic acquisition and customer-initiated referrals.
- PMF survey among appropriately experienced target users.
- Interview evidence of irreplaceable value.
- Support demand, feature requests, and willingness to accept tradeoffs.
- Market size and ability to reach similar users.

Do not use one threshold alone. A small devoted cohort may indicate segment fit but not a large or profitable market.

## Phase 6: scale responsibly

Scale only the unit that has been demonstrated:

- One segment.
- One promise.
- One product path.
- One channel.
- One geography or language.
- One economic model.

Increase spend or surface area gradually. Monitor retained CAC, payback, support load, infrastructure contribution, refund/fraud rate, moderation load, and product quality. Stop expansion when marginal cohorts degrade beyond the predeclared bound.

## Product-type branches

### Consumer mobile app

Prioritize store-page conversion, time to first value, D1/D7/D30 or natural-interval retention, crash-free sessions, review timing, privacy, and source-level downloads. Test screenshot and preview variants through App Store product-page optimization.

### B2B SaaS

Separate user, champion, buyer, security approver, and budget owner. Validate with workflow observation, paid pilot, activation to business outcome, account retention, expansion, gross margin, sales time, and payback. Founder-led outreach may outperform broad content before the ICP stabilizes.

### Developer tool or open-source product

Make the first successful result fast and reproducible. Treat documentation, examples, integrations, package registries, GitHub, and technical communities as product surfaces. Track successful setup and repeat use, not stars alone. Consider open source as trust and distribution only when the commercial model is explicit.

### Network, social, or marketplace product

Define the smallest atomic network and local liquidity metric. Make one-player value possible when feasible. Seed authentic supply/content, respond to newcomers, and measure successful matches or meaningful interactions per cohort. Broad user count is weaker than dense local utility.

### Creator product or information product

Validate the transformation and audience trust before production scale. Use useful free proof, a clear paid outcome, refund/support economics, completion, repeat purchase, and referrals. Disclose affiliate and sponsored relationships.

## Weekly operating cadence

Use a simple solo-founder rhythm:

- **Monday:** review cohorts, economics, support, and experiment results; choose one constraint.
- **Tuesday:** conduct interviews or inspect behavior; write the hypothesis and assets.
- **Wednesday:** launch one bounded experiment or product repair.
- **Thursday:** talk to users, support the seed cohort, and document qualitative evidence.
- **Friday:** review early safety signals without prematurely declaring a winner; publish useful learning if appropriate.
- **Monthly:** reconcile revenue, fees, variable cost, founder time, channel cohorts, and the evidence ledger; continue, revise, pause, or stop.

Protect time for product value and customer contact. Content volume is not progress when activation or retention is broken.

## 1. Source-video method

The source video, **「個人開発のネタ切れを終わらせる、AIアイデア発想術を紹介します【Claude/ChatGPT】」**, presents this practical sequence:

1. Treat an idea as a combination of elements rather than a flash of inspiration.
2. Ask an AI assistant to interview the founder about experience, interests, and daily behavior.
3. Choose a familiar activity such as tasks or note-taking.
4. Cross it with dimensions such as input method, recall, sharing, constraints, emotion, context, reduction, or AI assistance.
5. Select one combination, elaborate the concept, and create a visual mockup.
6. Show the concept publicly or through ads, collect reactions or waitlist sign-ups, and build quickly if the signal appears promising.

The video's note-taking example combines a memo behavior with emotional context and produces a mood-stamp diary concept. Its core contribution is a repeatable prompt-assisted workflow, not proof that the resulting concept has demand.

The method is described as a “2×2 matrix,” but the demonstrated technique is closer to a **morphological matrix**: define dimensions, list alternatives for each dimension, and combine compatible alternatives into candidate concepts. Do not force it into a literal two-axis, four-cell grid.

## 2. What the method gets right

### Start from founder-specific material

Personal experience helps the founder notice details, judge awkward workflows, reach credible early users, and persist through implementation. It also reduces the chance of producing only trend-shaped generic ideas.

Treat this as an advantage in **insight and access**, not evidence that a market exists. A private irritation may be rare, low-consequence, or already solved well enough for everyone else.

### Externalize combination dimensions

A matrix makes dimensions explicit and increases deliberate variation. This is useful when a founder repeatedly generates cosmetic variants of the same product.

The valuable unit is not “novel combination.” It is a coherent relationship among actor, situation, problem, desired progress, mechanism, payer, and distribution.

### Produce artifacts early

A written concept, storyboard, or mockup exposes ambiguity and makes a candidate easier to discuss. It is a learning artifact, not a demand metric.

### Move toward observable feedback

Showing a proposition to target users is stronger than privately debating it. The video correctly pushes the founder toward contact with the world instead of endless internal ideation.

## 3. Claims that need qualification

### “AI will produce better ideas”

Generative AI can increase output volume and improve average judged quality under some conditions. Research also finds convergence: people assisted by the same models may generate more similar ideas. Preserve unaided ideas, use independent passes, vary inputs intentionally, and cluster duplicates before evaluation.

### “A mockup, social response, or ad validates the idea”

These tests primarily measure whether a message earns attention from the sampled audience under a particular creative and channel. They do not establish problem severity, product use, retention, willingness to pay, acceptable acquisition cost, or sustainable operations.

Record the exact signal:

- A like is lightweight social response.
- A click is message and targeting response.
- An email is a small future-intent commitment.
- A qualified interview can reveal past behavior.
- Observed workflow or imported data shows deeper engagement.
- A deposit, preorder, or signed pilot adds economic commitment.
- Repeated use and paid retention test recurring value.

### “Build it in one week”

A short deadline can reduce overbuilding, but it is not universally responsible. Account for privacy, security, health or financial claims, platform review, accessibility, data rights, moderation, measurement, and support. Prefer “one week to the next valid learning artifact” over “one week to a public production launch.”

### “The idea is now the valuable part”

An idea alone rarely explains durable value. Evidence, distribution, execution, trust, accumulated data, workflow integration, switching cost, community, and repeated learning often matter more. Do not confuse an attractive description with an advantage.

### “Copying is the main threat”

Easy imitation can matter, but it is only one risk. More common early risks include weak problem demand, unreachable users, poor retention, unattractive economics, founder fatigue, platform dependence, and operational burden. Ask what improves as the product is used rather than relying on secrecy.

## 4. Research synthesis

### Problems are discovered through situated experience

Paul Graham's startup-idea guidance argues for noticing problems, especially problems the founder has, rather than trying to manufacture impressive-sounding startup ideas. Translate that principle into evidence: identify a specific actor, triggering situation, substitute, and consequence, then check whether others demonstrate the same behavior.

### Morphological methods support systematic combination

Morphological analysis decomposes a design space into parameters and alternatives, then examines compatible configurations. It is valuable for structured divergence, especially when the dimensions reflect a real problem. It does not select a market or replace feasibility and demand research.

### AI creates a quality–diversity tradeoff

Experimental research on generative AI and ideation suggests that AI can improve individual outputs while reducing collective diversity. A separate stream of human–AI ideation research supports hybrid processes where generation and evaluation are staged. Therefore:

1. Capture founder ideas before exposing them to model outputs.
2. Generate from different evidence packets rather than one growing context.
3. Use structured variation operators.
4. Cluster semantic duplicates.
5. Evaluate only after divergence.
6. Do not ask the same model to create, score, and certify its own ideas as market facts.

### Discovery and delivery require different evidence

The Design Council's Double Diamond separates divergent exploration from convergent definition and repeats this shape for solutions. Apply the same discipline here: first expand and define the problem landscape, then expand and evaluate possible responses. Moving between stages is allowed, but label the stage so that a polished solution does not erase an uncertain problem.

### Failure research favors testing market need

Startup-failure research repeatedly identifies absent or weak market need among major failure categories. Treat this as a reason to seek behavioral and economic evidence early, not as a universal percentage or deterministic rule.

## 5. Operational rules

- Preserve the source video's founder interview and combination method.
- Rename the demonstrated matrix accurately when teaching it.
- Separate founder fit from customer demand.
- Separate divergence, convergence, and validation.
- Require at least one evidence-led generation pass.
- Add an unsexy-problem pass to counter visual consumer-app bias.
- Label every signal by what it actually measures.
- Use hard gates before weighted scores.
- Select a cheap disconfirmation test, not merely a promotional asset.
- Hand a selected opportunity to `$indie-product-marketing`; do not keep generating indefinitely.

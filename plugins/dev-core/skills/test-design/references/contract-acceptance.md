# Contract acceptance

Use the governing agreement and existing accepted scope. For a new contract-planning
task, prepare acceptance criteria early enough for the owner and client to agree
the pass/fail boundary before committing to delivery. Do not turn an already agreed
implementation request into a new contract negotiation.

Draft the test plan with target features, relevant perspectives, environments,
exclusions, exit criteria, and risk priorities. Trace the acceptance specification
to the agreed criteria. Prepare the requested package using current decisions,
label proposals and unresolved scope explicitly, and reserve final contractual
agreement or material waivers for the authorized owner.

Use these fields where the delivery format does not already define them:

- **Specification:** case ID, linked acceptance criterion, preconditions, inputs
  and steps, expected result, environment.
- **Result report:** execution date, executor, environment and revision, per-case
  result, linked logs/screenshots or other evidence, failed items and remediation,
  open issues and recorded agreements.

Keep unexecuted, blocked, failed, and passed cases distinct. Do not fill a result
report from a plan, generated mock, or implementation claim. Before delivery,
execute the authorized acceptance checks or state what remains unverified; a
client's acceptance requires its own evidence and cannot be inferred from passing CI.

Use current primary sources and qualified counsel for legal clauses, liability,
or acceptance-law conclusions. Keep pricing and contractual scope decisions in
the owner's process. Preparing documents does not authorize sending them externally
or claiming the client received or signed them.
